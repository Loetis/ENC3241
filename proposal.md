# Proposal

Evaluating Cybersecurity Awareness and Phishing Vulnerability Among College Students
Eric DelRosario
Department of Information Technology
Background
Cybersecurity threats have become increasingly prevalent as digital technologies expand into
everyday life. One of the most common forms of cybercrime is phishing, where attackers
attempt to deceive individuals into revealing sensitive information such as passwords or
financial data. College students are particularly vulnerable due to frequent email usage, online
platforms, and limited formal cybersecurity training.
Recent studies show that human error remains one of the largest contributors to security
breaches, with phishing attacks accounting for a significant portion of successful cyber
intrusions (Verizon, 2023). Research also suggests that while students may be aware of
cybersecurity risks, they often lack the practical skills to identify and respond to threats (Sheng
et al., 2010). Understanding how students perceive and respond to phishing attempts can help
improve cybersecurity education and reduce risks.
This project aims to evaluate cybersecurity awareness among undergraduate students and
measure their susceptibility to phishing attempts through survey data and simulated scenarios.
Methodology
This study will use a mixed-methods approach combining surveys and simulated phishing
assessments:
● Participant Recruitment:
Recruit 30–50 undergraduate students from various majors at UCF.
● Survey Data Collection:
Participants will complete a questionnaire assessing:
○ Knowledge of cybersecurity concepts
○ Previous exposure to phishing attempts
○ Confidence in identifying threats
● Phishing Simulation:
Participants will be shown a series of mock emails (legitimate and phishing examples)
and asked to identify whether each is safe or malicious.
● Data Analysis:
○ Quantitative analysis of correct vs. incorrect identifications
○ Correlation between self-reported knowledge and actual performance
○ Identification of common mistakes and patterns
All data will be anonymized and collected in compliance with ethical research standards.
Anticipated Outcomes
The project is expected to reveal gaps between perceived and actual cybersecurity awareness
among students. It will likely show that while many students feel confident in identifying phishing
attempts, their actual detection accuracy may be lower.
Significance / Use of the Project
This research will contribute to improving cybersecurity education strategies by identifying
specific areas where students struggle. The findings can inform universities on how to design
more effective training programs.
